A tutor that finds why you keep getting it wrong.

Most learners aren’t missing facts. One wrong idea in their head keeps causing the same kind of mistake. Rootcause finds that idea and helps fix it.

Perceptron Hackathon 2026, Theme 5: AI That Teaches, Problem 8

Scroll to follow the idea.

Saying “wrong” doesn’t help

Most apps mark an answer wrong and hand you more questions. But every orange block you see here is a different mistake caused by the same hidden idea, so more practice changes nothing.

Example: a student works out 1/3 + 1/4 and gets 2/7. They are adding the tops and adding the bottoms separately.

Step 1: guess the hidden idea

An AI model (Gemma) writes small programs. Each one acts out a different wrong idea a student might have.

We run them all on the student’s answer. Only the programs that give the exact same wrong answer stay lit. The rest go dark.

Step 2: ask the question that splits the guesses

Two guesses can give the same answer to one question. So we pick the next question where the guesses give different answers.

The student’s reply tells us which guess is right, using as few questions as possible.

Step 3: show them why it breaks

Once we are confident, we show a case where the student’s own idea gives an absurd result, like 1/2 + 1/2 = 1/2.

The broken idea gets replaced by the right one, and a fresh question checks that it stuck.

Why this is different

  1. Nothing is hand-written. The AI writes the wrong ideas, so the tutor isn’t limited to a short fixed list.
  2. Everything is checked. A guess only counts if the code reproduces the student’s answer, and answer keys come from a math solver.
  3. It admits doubt. When no guess fits, it says “not sure” instead of making something up.

How we will prove it works

We test with simulated students who each have a known wrong idea, and measure three things:

  1. How often it finds the right idea.
  2. How few questions it needs.
  3. Whether targeted practice fixes the mistake faster than random practice.

The tests are simulations and we say so. A small pilot with friends is our stretch goal.

What we deliver for Problem 8

The must-haves

  1. Sort a wrong answer into one of five clear categories. The AI finds the categories from student mistakes, then we name the five most common.
  2. Make a new practice question aimed at that category, with the answer checked by a math solver.

The stretch goals

  1. Remember each student’s mistakes across sessions in a small database.
  2. Show that targeted practice reduces the mistake, first with simulated students, then a small pilot with friends.

We also check our categories against real student errors from ASSISTments.

Built with Antigravity

Five small jobs, one agent each, each with a check that says it works:

  1. A safe runner and answer checker
  2. The wrong-idea writer
  3. The idea grouper
  4. The question picker
  5. The app, with photo input

Free open tools, mostly Google: Gemma 3, EmbeddingGemma, Google ADK, SymPy, Gradio.
